# Edge Inventory — "Where does Will have edge?"

**Purpose:** the single prep artifact for the **Aug 24 quarterly**, whose headline question is
this one. Built 2026-07-27, a month early and deliberately: the 7/26 reflection recorded three
acquisition-direction changes in five days, each following podcast consumption rather than new
evidence. Assembling the evidence *before* the decision date is the anti-oscillation mechanism —
the decision gets made once, from this, with `references/hormozi.md`'s edge test.

**This document does not decide.** Per the `edge-inventory-quarterly-prep` backlog row, no
vertical decisions happen before Aug 24. It inventories what's real, names what's missing, and
enumerates candidate shapes without picking one.

Sources: `context/work-experience.md` (capability inventory, self-rated), `context/acquisition.md`
(Zone of Genius), `references/hormozi.md` (edge test), `journals/acquisition-log.md` (9 evals).

## The core finding: this is a monetization gap, not a skills gap

Will asked earlier this month what AI-age-relevant skills he should build. His own capability file
already answers it: **he has them, at 9/10, with receipts.** Six-workflow production GenAI suite
(~407 hr/yr recovered), Context Document methodology taught to 11 stakeholders, deterministic
gating logic, Claude-Code-native tooling that a teammate ran off a three-word prompt during his
OOTO. That is not a beginner's portfolio.

What has never happened: **a stranger has paid him for any of it.** Every artifact he's produced
was compensated by salary, not bought. Hormozi's validation bar — *a stranger hands you money* —
has never been tested against Will's actual edge.

That single fact explains both low wheel axes at once:

- **Growth 4/10.** The rubric rates artifacts produced, and its 3–4 band reads *"the skill named
  as 'the edge' plateaued another quarter."* The skills are real but they've only ever been
  exercised inside one employer's walls, against one employer's problems.
- **Mission 4/10.** "Where do I have edge" feels unanswerable because the edge has never been
  priced. Ambiguity here isn't a thinking problem; it's a missing data point.

**The implication for a side venture:** the first one's job is not income. It's to convert a
9/10 internal capability into one external dollar — the cheapest possible test of whether the
edge survives contact with a market.

## The master filter: Will's edge is supply-side, not demand-side

Every 9/10 is about **making an existing operation cheaper, faster, or more codified.** Every cold
zone is about **creating demand that doesn't exist yet.** That asymmetry is the sharpest filter
available, and it retro-explains a lot of decisions that were made on instinct:

- **Buying > building** — an acquired business inherits its demand. Will's file says it directly:
  "most boring businesses have hours/week of human judgment locked inside the seller's head."
  Buying puts his 9/10 to work on day one and never tests his 2/10.
- **Why every "start a thing" idea has felt wrong** — content plays, e-commerce, audience plays
  all put the 2/10 on the critical path.
- **Hormozi's warm-outreach-first sequencing isn't just good advice here, it's the workaround.**
  Channel #1 (warm 1:1) generates demand through relationships rather than marketing skill —
  the one demand channel that doesn't route through the gap.

**Rule to carry into Aug 24:** *if a venture requires Will to manufacture demand from strangers,
it is fighting his weakest skill. If it requires him to take over, codify, renegotiate, or
systematize demand that already exists, it is playing his strongest.*

## Hormozi edge test, applied

- **"Use what you got."** Domain exposure that's already deep: supply chain & inbound logistics,
  transportation rate structures, carrier/3PL contract dynamics, ops data infrastructure, AI
  tooling for operations, business-review mechanism design. A venture inside that surface starts
  with ignorance debt already substantially paid down.
- **Debt taxonomy.** Ignorance debt is lowest in B2B ops/logistics/automation and highest in
  demand-gen. Financial debt must never stack on top of ignorance debt — so any first venture in
  a *new* domain should carry ~zero financial debt, while a venture inside his existing domain can
  tolerate structure (the deal-#1 doctrine: cash + non-recourse seller note only).
- **Finite tries.** The more you need the win, the higher-probability the bet. Will doesn't need
  this one — Amazon income is intact — so a *low-cost, high-information* first bet is correct
  regardless of ceiling.
- **Stacking / 3–5 year seasons.** The next thing should build on the last or the domain-knowledge
  clock restarts. Supply-chain ops + AI-enabled mechanism-building is the stack already in
  progress; the promo doc is its receipt.
- **Network as an asset (already held):** Dan (multi-business owner, connector), Richard Lobo
  (trucking), Jake Dillard (payroll), Jin (real estate portfolio), Mikey Shudoma (bank ops / SBA).
  **Every one of those is a small-business owner or operator with ops problems** — i.e. the warm
  channel is not hypothetical, it's five named doors.
- **Elena.** CFO/COO profile (modeling, technical reporting, program management, process docs, QC).
  Complements the supply-side edge; does not fill the demand-side gap.

## Candidate shapes — enumerated, NOT picked

Scored on the master filter. Aug 24 chooses; this only frames the choice.

**1. Productized AI-ops service sold into his own network.**
*Edge fit: highest.* Uses the 9/10 automation + 8/10 financial-framing directly; demand comes via
warm channel (five named owners), not marketing. Near-zero capital, so no financial-on-ignorance
debt. Fastest possible route to the missing data point — one stranger paying for the edge.
*Honest risk:* the 4/10 offer-construction gap is on the critical path for pricing, and delivery
time competes with the promo window. Ceiling is unclear until validated.

**2. Acquire a small B2B service business, re-pointed at his edge.**
*Edge fit: high.* This is the original thesis minus the finance vertical (which was Dan's edge,
never Will's). His file names the target profile: recurring service businesses where dispatching,
route optimization, and quote-to-invoice are the bottlenecks. Inherits demand entirely. **Material
already built:** 620 ranked solo-track leads across 8 surviving sectors — specialty distribution
(170 leads, "Will SCM home turf"), pest control (117), HOA management (114). The parked
`zog-sector-pick` row is this candidate's next step.
*Honest risk:* the 3/10 deal-sourcing skill, longest time-to-close, and the debt doctrine
constrains structure hard.

**3. Fractional ops / automation consulting.**
*Edge fit: high, ceiling low.* Same skills as #1, sold by the hour or the project rather than
productized. Fastest validation, most capped, least stacking value.
*Honest risk:* trades time for money — structurally opposed to the truly-absentee end state in
`context/acquisition.md`.

**4. Anything demand-gen-first** (content, audience, e-commerce, media).
*Edge fit: lowest — fails the master filter by construction.* Recorded here so it doesn't get
re-proposed as novel. Dan's media instinct belongs downstream of an operating business, as the
growth channel (Hormozi #2 layered on #5/#8), not as the venture itself.

   as the promo window where acquisition activity must not compromise day-job behavior. Whatever
   Aug 24 picks has to fit *that* constraint, not an idealized one.
