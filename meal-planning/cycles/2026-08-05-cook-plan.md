## RETRO — Curry (#42), cooked Thu 8/6 (a day late; was scheduled Wed 8/5)

**Outcome: not creamy enough, not flavorful enough, not spicy enough.** All three trace to a
single root cause — **the masala never actually cooked** (the *bhuna* step). Everything
downstream inherited a raw, thin base.

**What went wrong in the moment:**
- Paste came out of the blender as a thick *pulp*, not a paste, and never simmered, never
  tightened, never lost the raw-onion smell across 4 min → 3 more at medium → 2 more.
- **The Dutch oven lid was on.** That was the immediate killer — trapped steam condenses back
  in, so the paste can't evaporate, can't thicken, can't brown.
- Heat ran low-medium or below for essentially the whole cook. The lentil simmer was too gentle
  too: lentils went creamy but held disc shape past 15 min, and the pot only reduced ~20%.
- Liquid never thickened even after the chicken went in (chicken releases its own moisture,
  partially offsetting evaporation).

**Root causes found by diffing the adaptation against the original** (Dr. Rupy, *South Indian
Style Monkfish and Lentil Curry*, 4 servings — Will produced the source afterward):
| Failure | Cause |
|---|---|
| Not spicy | Original uses **2 tsp red chilli flakes soaked in boiling water**. Adaptation used 1.5 Tbsp "chili powder" — if American chili powder (paprika/cumin/oregano blend), it carries almost no heat |
| Not creamy | Original's **2 Tbsp desiccated coconut** is a *dry* thickener soaked in 100ml boiling water. The 8/5 cost-swap to skimmed coconut cream removed the only absorbent ingredient and added liquid instead |
| Watery | Original uses **no water at all**. Adaptation added 2.5 cups → coconut dropped from 57% to 43% of total liquid |
| Flat | Onion **blended raw** (and ~1.7× over-scaled per serving). Blended raw onion is mostly water and never develops; the original browns nothing but has 1/3 the onion and a smooth paste |
| Under-seasoned | 1 tsp salt for 7 servings. The original's own commenters report doubling the salt and adding more |
| Missing aromatics | **Star anise, curry leaves, whole cumin seeds, cilantro-to-serve** were all dropped entirely in adaptation |

**Fixes applied to recipe #42** (see its `notes` for the full changelog): onion diced and
browned first (never blended) · masala doneness cue made explicit (*thickens + pulls away from
the pot bottom*, lid OFF) · water → 0 with a ½ cup reserve, stock 2→3 cups · salt 1→2.5 tsp ·
soaked chilli flakes restored · desiccated coconut restored · star anise / curry leaves / whole
cumin / cilantro restored · garlic 8→11, mustard seeds and ginger re-scaled · lentil simmer
15→20 min at a *real* simmer · tomato paste + lime-juice-stirred-in added · spinach 7 cups→8oz
(was actually **under** the source ratio) · **chicken 22oz→44oz** to restore the original's
protein density (~46-50g/serving, but +9-10g fat/serving — half a Cut #2 day in one meal, so
trim the thighs and pair with lean days).

**Rejected after checking the source:** garam masala and kasoori methi — both North Indian; this
is a Keralan/Goan dish and the original contains neither. Will's McCormick curry powder is used
instead, **bloomed early** with the turmeric (it holds raw coriander/fenugreek seed that needs
cooking, unlike pre-toasted garam masala).

**Simplification hacks adopted 8/7** — canned crushed tomatoes replace 4 fresh (cuts prep *and*
fixes wateriness) · jarred ginger-garlic paste replaces 11 cloves + ginger · **make the paste the
night before** (keeps 2-3 days) · batch-brown onions and freeze in ~175g pucks. Net: cooking-day
prep drops to ~8 min. Declined: immersion blender purchase, dropping the rice.

**Standing lessons beyond this recipe:**
1. **Lid off for any reduction or browning step.** This one mistake caused most of the cascade.
2. **"Light simmer" needs visible bubbles across the surface**, not just steam. Low heat was the
   through-line of every symptom.
3. When adapting a recipe, **check what the substitution was doing structurally** — swapping a
   dry thickener for a liquid fat isn't a like-for-like cost save.
