# William Zhou — L4 → L5 Promo Doc

William Zhou joined Amazon in October 2024 as a Supply Chain Manager I within Inbound Planning and Execution (IPEX). Within six months he owned redirects for two of five Regional Inbound Cross-Dock (rIXD) regions — and, beyond that standard redirect-execution scope, architected ICT's daily-through-quarterly business-review system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR). In Q3 2025 he expanded to owning all five rIXD regions, and in Q1 2026 took on National IXD (nIXD) as well. **Today, William owns 1DC + rIXD + nIXD redirect execution for IPEX-Inbound Control Tower (ICT) — 55 rIXD/nIXD sites (35 rIXD + 20 nIXD, as of July 2026) with ~450M units/week of rIXD+nIXD inbound arrivals across those sites (WK28 2026 actual), plus the 1DC network and the launching RDC network (IMO1, XLA4).**

The following examples demonstrate consistent L5 performance: navigating ambiguous cross-functional problems with minimal direction, codifying judgment into reusable mechanisms rather than handing off recommendations, and reaching consensus with internal and external partner teams on new cross-org processes.

# Scope of Role

The Supply Chain Manager II (SCMII) **1DC + Regional IXD (rIXD) + National IXD (nIXD) Redirect Execution** role within IPEX-Inbound Control Tower (ICT) is an individual-contributor scope where the rules for daily reactive steering live as tribal knowledge across senior peers. The role demands command of constraint-level MDP (per-constraint backlog days), Tagawa buffer, Gap-to-Goal backlog, and yard utilization to guide tactical stakeholders (Site Operations, Supply Chain Execution (SCE), Smart Reroute), lead escalations reaching Director-level review, and push back with data on senior asks that shift the problem rather than solve it. When a multi-week redirect pattern stems from upstream demand misplacement rather than genuine capacity constraint, the SCMII flags it, pushes upstream levers, and declines the redirect — protecting the network from bullwhip.

**The SCMII owns 1DC + rIXD + nIXD redirect execution across 55 live rIXD/nIXD sites and ~450M units/week of inbound arrivals across those sites, within a 2026 redirect budget of $36.3M ($20.4M New Vendor Freight (NVF) inbound + $15.9M New Transfer In (NTI))** — extending forward to RDC launch volume (IMO1, XLA4) as those sites ramp. **The role codifies the rules of reactive steering into tooling and standards — hours of morning human narrative into minutes of output, tribal SIM-vetting judgment into deterministic criterion gates — delegating tactical work to tooling and teammates, optimizing for disciplined redirect execution within budget.** It holds internal and external partners (SCE, Carrier Management, J.B. Hunt Intermodal) to the same data bar before approving redirects, and autonomously surfaces risky plans for further review. Finally, it requires high-**Quality**, high-**Speed**, high-**Tact** verbal and written communication — demonstrated daily in business reviews and escalations.

**Example 4: ICT Business-Review Architecture — Building the DBR→QBR Performance-Measurement System from Scratch**

**In his first six months at Amazon, William architected ICT's daily-through-quarterly performance-measurement system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR) — a 49-plus-metric taxonomy, its ETL pipeline, and its IPEX Analytics publishing — that became the redirect-execution metric of record the steering org reads from.** When William joined, ICT had no codified performance-measurement cadence for redirect execution: results were read ad hoc, with no standard definition of on-time, no defect attribution, and no daily-to-quarterly reporting rhythm. He designed the system end to end. The metric taxonomy spans execution (created, cancelled, planned, uncovered VRIDs, picked-up, delivered, cost), on-time pickup and delivery quality, and defect attribution by controllability (FC- vs IPEX- vs carrier- vs uncontrollable), each broken out by network (National/Regional IXD, Sortable, Non-Sort) and mode (truckload, 1P/3P intermodal, LTL), with the weekly grain adding lead-time-savings components — projected-vs-actual transit, dwell, and pickup delay — to isolate where redirect lead time is lost. He owned the analytical hard parts personally: correcting the on-time logic for loads scheduled at a week boundary but picked up the prior week, excluding freight modes whose EDI gaps corrupted the on-time signal, and re-basing transfer dwell to align with vendor-redirect dwell at hourly grain. When a source table stopped populating the load-creator field at the daily grain — which would have made it impossible to separate IPEX redirects from other aliases on the shared shipper account, breaking day-over-day tracking of the team's core function — William wrote the problem statement, escalated it, and drove the fix to a validated alternate table. Setting the roadmap and per-workstream owners, he coordinated IPEX Analytics on the ETL build and IBWR on the data workaround, and drove the ICT-management sign-off and quarterly-review gate — taking the system from first definition to a live, auto-published DBR feeding the weekly-review deck and the quarterly business review. The judgment later encoded in his flash automation (Example 3) was first structured in these reviews — he built the measurement foundation, then automated the daily narrative that reads from it.
