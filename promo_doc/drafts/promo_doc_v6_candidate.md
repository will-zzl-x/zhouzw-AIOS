# William Zhou — L4 → L5 Promo Doc

William Zhou joined Amazon in October 2024 as a Supply Chain Manager I within Inbound Planning and Execution (IPEX). Within six months he owned redirects for two of five Regional Inbound Cross-Dock (rIXD) regions and had built ICT's daily-through-quarterly business-review system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR) from scratch. By Q3 2025 he owned all five rIXD regions; in Q1 2026 he added National IXD (nIXD). **Today, William owns 1DC + rIXD + nIXD redirect execution for IPEX-Inbound Control Tower (ICT) — 55 rIXD/nIXD sites (35 rIXD + 20 nIXD, as of July 2026) with ~450M units/week of rIXD+nIXD inbound arrivals across those sites (WK28 2026 actual), plus the 1DC network, the launching RDC network (IMO1, XLA4), and out-of-network launch-support redirects for the Specialty (SDC) network (HGR5, QXX6).**

The examples below show consistent L5 performance: he works ambiguous cross-functional problems with minimal direction, turns team judgment into tools and standards the team runs, and reaches consensus with internal and external partners on new cross-org processes.

# Scope of Role

The Supply Chain Manager II (SCMII) **1DC + Regional IXD (rIXD) + National IXD (nIXD) Redirect Execution** role within IPEX-Inbound Control Tower (ICT) is an individual-contributor scope where the rules for daily reactive steering live as tribal knowledge across senior peers. The role demands command of constraint-level MDP (per-constraint backlog days), Tagawa buffer, Gap-to-Goal backlog, and yard utilization to guide tactical stakeholders (Site Operations, Supply Chain Execution (SCE), Smart Reroute), lead escalations reaching Director-level review, and push back with data on senior asks that shift the problem rather than solve it. When a multi-week redirect pattern stems from upstream demand misplacement rather than genuine capacity constraint, the SCMII flags it, pushes upstream levers, and declines the redirect — protecting the network from bullwhip.

**The SCMII owns 1DC + rIXD + nIXD redirect execution across 55 live rIXD/nIXD sites and ~450M units/week of inbound arrivals across those sites, within a 2026 redirect budget of $36.3M ($20.4M New Vendor Freight (NVF) inbound + $15.9M New Transfer In (NTI))** — extending forward to RDC launch volume (IMO1, XLA4) as those sites ramp. Since February 2026 the role has also served as ICT's point of ownership for **out-of-network SDC launch support** — feeding pre-launch NVF test freight into new-launch Specialty sites (HGR5, then QXX6 from May) so they validate receiving before go-live — owning the redirect-candidate analytics the pilots select from and the cross-network redirect governance they execute against. **The role also turns the rules of reactive steering into tooling and standards: the morning narrative that took hours to build by hand now generates in minutes, and SIM vetting that ran on individual judgment now runs through set criteria. That frees the role to delegate tactical work to tooling and teammates and keep redirect execution within budget.** It holds internal and external partners (SCE, Carrier Management, J.B. Hunt Intermodal) to the same high bar on data before approving redirects, and autonomously surfaces risky plans for further review. Finally, it requires clear, fast, and tactful written and verbal communication — demonstrated daily in business reviews and escalations.

**Example 1: 3P Intermodal Rate Restructure — Cross-Functional Cost Methodology with J.B. Hunt Intermodal Pricing Leadership**

**William led the cross-functional restructure of 3P intermodal redirect pricing — converting a 2–4-hour per-request spot-quote negotiation into a published, auditable standard that cut cost per move by a verified 47%.** The methodology delivered $79K in rate savings across its first 359 redirects ($229K projected annualized) and now governs every 3P-IM redirect the team prices — 324 year-to-date (~$85K in estimated rate savings). In Q3 2025, third-party intermodal redirects ran on a spot-quote model that exposed Amazon to a Finance-estimated ~$2M in historical overpayment — carriers receiving double payment for overlapping transportation segments. Over 3+ months of negotiation with J.B. Hunt Intermodal (HJBI) pricing leadership, Carrier Services, AP, and Controllership, William restructured the methodology so Amazon pays only incremental costs rather than the full lane: he secured a foundational rate-publication agreement (quarterly rate reviews with published ramp-to-FC linehaul rates), then drove four weeks of methodology iteration to a rate-difference-plus-administrative-fee structure that addressed HJBI's concerns about being compensated while keeping Amazon's payment limited to incremental costs. He reached alignment with HJBI's pricing leadership on the core methodology and paired it with an automated rate-lookup tool so pricing no longer waits on a rate request — a standard his live tracker runs against, re-verified in July 2026 against the published rate matrix.

**Example 2: Operationalizing GenAI into Production Operations — the Flash of Record and the Redirect Decision-Tooling Suite**

**In late 2025 — when the sanctioned AI tooling was chatbot-only, before any CLI- or IDE-based tools existed in the org — William adopted and operationalized the Context Document methodology (a structured way of teaching an AI tool your role constraints and decision frameworks so the same context isn't re-explained every task) and used it to take GenAI somewhere it had not yet been taken: governed, test-guarded production tooling the team runs as its mechanism of record.** Two builds anchor the record. First, the daily IPEX Ops Flash: in H1 2025 the flash was hand-assembled each afternoon from dashboards, exports, and a steering doc — a ~2-hour combined build, with multiple network owners each spending 15–30+ minutes on their sections plus assembly of the narrative. William mapped the whole problem end-to-end (strategy, operational rules, data shape, execution) and rebuilt it with GenAI-assisted development into an automated pipeline that generates the network-steering narrative in minutes — **roughly 500 hours a year of combined manager capacity returned**, every network produced from a single pipeline, and every recurring call in the prior human narrative now an explicit, repeatable rule. It is the team's flash of record, anchored by an internal wiki he wrote and owns, and it ran through a teammate's coverage during his May 2026 out-of-office without him.

Second, he extended the same pattern into a redirect decision-tooling suite that turns the team's vetting, costing, and audit judgment into deterministic code: a **redirect validator** — a five-criterion warrant gate (three giver-side, two taker-side) that returns a PASS/FAIL where SIM vetting previously ran on individual judgment, with a self-inflicted-backlog check that catches redirects driven by planned-in receipts before they consume redirect budget; a **cost estimator** — a per-lane cost-per-load model built on trailing 60-day actuals and resolved through the geographic procurement-lane hierarchy, so cost is checked before a redirect is approved instead of after the money is spent, directly protecting the $36.3M redirect budget; and a **SIM auditor** that reconciles executed redirect SIMs against the steering tracker, catching mis-booked levers and phantom-zero execution that manual review missed.

What makes this GenAI *operationalized* rather than GenAI *used*: the suite is version-controlled and **test-guarded (1,100+ automated tests)** — AI-assisted code held to the same regression bar as any production mechanism — and it ships as a shared team package with onboarding documentation that teammates build from without him, including Kiro-based onboarding for teammates. He then taught the approach outward, **training 11 stakeholders across IPEX on the Context Document methodology**. Every recurring redirect decision — is it warranted, what does it cost, did it actually execute — now runs on an explicit, auditable rule instead of individual judgment.

**Leadership Principle(s) demonstrated:** Learn and Be Curious, Invent and Simplify, Ownership, Deliver Results
*Role guideline: "You identify priorities and know when a situation requires a deep dive vs. issues that can be solved quickly."*

**Example 3: Low-Cost Redirect Levers — Midstream Intermodal Reroute & Origin-Side Flip**

**When a mid-2026 Finance/ATS trailer-cap directive tightened the redirect-eligibility distance gate (1,000→750 miles) and cut redirect volume ~12%, William built new, lower-cost redirect levers that recover the freight at points in the transportation cycle where Amazon pays only incremental cost.** Redirect execution had effectively one mechanism: a full downstream re-move once freight reached a stressed node. William looked for cheaper intervention points at both ends of the load lifecycle:

- **AZIM 1P intermodal midstream reroute (in-transit).** Rather than wait for an intermodal load to arrive and then pay a full downstream redirect, William reroutes it *while it is still on the rail leg* to a taker that needs the work — Amazon pays only the difference between the re-pointed final-mile dray and the original one, and avoids the full giver→taker over-the-road re-move entirely. He wrote the reroutability logic himself — rail-tier, steel-wheel-exclusion, standard-vs-crosstown (the divert window closes when a load departs its rail-interchange crosstown), and chassis-zone gates that determine whether an in-transit load can still be legally and physically diverted — and set up the request-and-execution process with the Amazon Intermodal (AZIM) team. Since launching in WK24 the lever has redirected **323 in-transit loads / 5.39M units (~46/week)** for a **net transport avoidance of ~$137K to date — ~$820K on an annualized basis (scaled to the seasonal redirect-volume curve, not a flat run-rate)** — priced load-by-load against carrier settlement actuals, with the avoided rail continuation on ramp-change flips measured from its own canceled rail leg rather than estimated (conservative floor ~$109K if those canceled legs were somehow still billed; current-week in-flight loads remain estimated pending accrual). The mechanism at its best: 28 loads bound for a Carolina rail ramp were intercepted at the Chicago crosstown and trucked straight to the Toledo taker — so the re-pointed load runs a shorter rail structure *and* skips the downstream truck move, on every load. **The same midstream principle carries to 3P intermodal (HJBI/PGLI) loads**, where an in-transit rail reroute likewise substitutes an incremental leg for a full OTR redirect.
- **OB NTI origin-side flip (pre-dispatch).** At the opposite end of the cycle, William drove the outbound New-Transfer-In flip — redirecting transfer freight at the *origin* before a truck ever dispatches (nIXD → rIXD FC2), the cheapest possible intervention point — from an inconsistent break-glass action into a documented standard lever, reaching agreement with Reactive Scheduling and Amazon Transportation Services (ATS) on the mechanics and eligibility.
- **TOFC over OTR — mechanism choice, executed.** He backs lever selection with unit economics: the LAS1 Trailer-on-Flatcar (TOFC) teardown priced the same freight at **$2.6K/trailer railed via TOFC vs $6.6K over-the-road — ~$4K/trailer saved by railing it instead of trucking it** (and ~$1.7K/trailer below the incumbent LAS1 routing). The Q2 LAS1 TOFC program adopted this routing, choosing the cheapest feasible path per lane rather than defaulting to OTR.

Told to move less, William found ways to move the same freight for less money — and wrote each lever's eligibility rules so the team runs them as standard levers rather than re-deriving them per load. The same midstream playbook extends next to Amazon Freight External (AFE) freight: an AFE midstream reroute is the next lever in build.

**Leadership Principle(s) demonstrated:** Invent and Simplify, Frugality, Are Right, A Lot, Deliver Results
*Role guideline: "You effectively manage difficult problems, mitigate immediate risks, and decide when you can handle or need to escalate."*

**Example 4: ICT Business-Review Architecture — Building the DBR→QBR Performance-Measurement System from Scratch**

**In his first six months at Amazon, William built ICT's daily-through-quarterly performance-measurement system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR) — a 49-plus-metric set, its ETL pipeline, and its IPEX Analytics publishing — that became the redirect-execution metric of record the steering org reads from.** When William joined, ICT had no codified performance-measurement cadence for redirect execution: results were read ad hoc, with no standard definition of on-time, no defect attribution, and no daily-to-quarterly reporting rhythm. He designed the system end to end. The metric taxonomy spans execution (created, cancelled, planned, uncovered VRIDs, picked-up, delivered, cost), on-time pickup and delivery quality, and defect attribution by controllability (FC- vs IPEX- vs carrier- vs uncontrollable), each broken out by network (National/Regional IXD, Sortable, Non-Sort) and mode (truckload, 1P/3P intermodal, LTL), with the weekly grain adding lead-time-savings components — projected-vs-actual transit, dwell, and pickup delay — to isolate where redirect lead time is lost. He did the hard analytical parts himself: correcting the on-time logic for loads scheduled at a week boundary but picked up the prior week, excluding freight modes whose EDI gaps corrupted the on-time signal, and re-basing transfer dwell to align with vendor-redirect dwell at hourly grain. When a source table stopped populating the load-creator field at the daily grain — which would have made it impossible to separate IPEX redirects from other aliases on the shared shipper account, breaking day-over-day tracking of the team's core function — William wrote the problem statement, escalated it, and drove the fix to a validated alternate table. Setting the roadmap and per-workstream owners, he coordinated IPEX Analytics on the ETL build and IBWR on the data workaround, and drove the ICT-management sign-off and quarterly-review gate — taking the system from first definition to a live, auto-published DBR feeding the weekly-review deck and the quarterly business review. These reviews defined the metrics the flash automation (Example 2) later reads from — he built the measurement foundation, then automated the daily narrative on top of it.

**Leadership Principle(s) demonstrated:** Invent and Simplify, Ownership, Dive Deep, Deliver Results
*Role guideline: "You work with partner teams to understand their roadmap and reach consensus on approach in order to deliver to meet business goals."*

2. **Codified judgment as scalable team tooling.** The GenAI record in Example 2 proves that owning a problem from strategy through execution converts hours of human judgment into governed automation the team adopts and runs without him — which scales redirect-execution capacity beyond one person. Extending it across more of the reactive-steering surface — the WBR narrative, SIM-approval vetting, and the redirectable and execution dashboards — is the direct continuation.

3. **Program-level cross-org partnership — with the HJBI Drop-to-Live pilot in flight.** The pilot was identified and sized by the IPEX-VSCOR team in November 2025 at $22.5M annualized and had stalled since; William re-engaged it to live execution within three weeks of taking primary — pivoting to the HJBL SCAC-split when he identified that the original conversion mechanism would break the carrier's drop first-pass yield — and launched it WK20, with the 2026 target held to $2.25M pending scalability read-out. He is driving that pilot forward now, alongside the OB NTI flip standardized with Reactive Scheduling and ATS — each a new cross-org process William set up or standardized end to end. At L5 he continues as the IPEX-IXD analytical owner extending that pattern across emerging redirect programs.

4. **Network-wide redirect standards and new-network execution.** William authored the IXD redirectability-feasibility intake for Amazon's network-wide redirect-automation program — the same rail-tier, standard-vs-crosstown, chassis-zone, and appointment-status logic he built for his own levers, generalized into the spec the automation validates against — and routed the per-network sign-off, proposed to the approving leaders. In parallel he executes redirects into networks that don't yet run on their own: pre-launch NVF test freight into the launching RDC network (IMO1, XLA4) and out-of-network SDC sites (HGR5, QXX6), owning the redirect-candidate analytics and the pre-appointment sibling/pre-ISA vetting those pilots select from. The L5 role formalizes that pattern — define what's redirectable, then stand up the execution wherever the redirect lands.
