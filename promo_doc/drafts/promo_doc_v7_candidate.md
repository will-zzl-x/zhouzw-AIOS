# William Zhou — L4 → L5 Promo Doc

William Zhou joined Amazon in October 2024 as a Supply Chain Manager I within Inbound Planning and Execution (IPEX). Within six months he owned redirects for two of five Regional Inbound Cross-Dock (rIXD) regions and had built ICT's daily-through-quarterly business-review system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR) from scratch; by Q3 2025 he owned all five rIXD regions, and in Q1 2026 he added National IXD (nIXD). **Today, William owns 1DC + rIXD + nIXD redirect execution for IPEX-Inbound Control Tower (ICT) — 55 rIXD/nIXD sites (35 rIXD + 20 nIXD, as of July 2026) carrying ~450M units/week of inbound arrivals (WK28 2026 actual), plus the 1DC network, the launching RDC network (IMO1, XLA4), and out-of-network launch-support redirects for the Specialty (SDC) network (HGR5, QXX6).**

The examples below show consistent L5 performance: he works ambiguous cross-functional problems with minimal direction, turns team judgment into tools and standards the team runs, and reaches consensus with internal and external partners on new cross-org processes.

---

# Scope of Role

The Supply Chain Manager II (SCMII) **1DC + Regional IXD (rIXD) + National IXD (nIXD) Redirect Execution** role within IPEX-Inbound Control Tower (ICT) is an individual-contributor scope where the rules for daily reactive steering live as tribal knowledge across senior peers. It demands command of constraint-level MDP, Tagawa buffer, Gap-to-Goal backlog, and yard utilization to guide tactical stakeholders (Site Operations, Supply Chain Execution (SCE), Smart Reroute), lead escalations reaching Director-level review, and push back with data on senior asks that shift the problem rather than solve it — declining multi-week redirect patterns that stem from upstream demand misplacement to protect against bullwhip.

**The SCMII owns 1DC + rIXD + nIXD redirect execution across 55 live rIXD/nIXD sites and ~450M units/week of inbound arrivals, within a 2026 redirect budget of $36.3M ($20.4M New Vendor Freight (NVF) inbound + $15.9M New Transfer In (NTI))** — extending forward to RDC launch volume (IMO1, XLA4) as those sites ramp. Since February 2026 it has also been ICT's point of ownership for **out-of-network SDC launch support**, feeding pre-launch NVF test freight into new Specialty sites (HGR5, then QXX6 from May) to validate receiving before go-live. It holds internal and external partners (SCE, Carrier Management, J.B. Hunt Intermodal) to the same high data bar before approving redirects, autonomously surfaces risky plans, and demands clear, fast, tactful communication in business reviews and escalations.

**Example 1: 3P Intermodal Rate Restructure — Cross-Functional Cost Methodology with J.B. Hunt Intermodal Pricing Leadership**

**William led the cross-functional restructure of 3P intermodal redirect pricing — converting a 2–4-hour-per-request spot-quote negotiation into a published, auditable standard that cut cost per move by a verified 47%.** In Q3 2025 these redirects ran on a spot-quote model that exposed Amazon to a Finance-estimated ~$2M in historical overpayment, with carriers double-paid for overlapping segments. Over 3+ months with J.B. Hunt Intermodal (HJBI) pricing leadership, Carrier Services, AP, and Controllership, he restructured the methodology so Amazon pays only incremental costs rather than the full lane: a foundational rate-publication agreement (quarterly reviews with published ramp-to-FC linehaul rates), then four weeks of iteration to a rate-difference-plus-administrative-fee structure that compensated HJBI while limiting Amazon to incremental costs — paired with an automated rate-lookup tool. It delivered **$79K in rate savings across its first 359 redirects ($229K projected annualized)** and now governs every 3P-IM redirect the team prices — **324 year-to-date, ~$85K in estimated rate savings**, re-verified in July 2026 against the published rate matrix.

**Leadership Principle(s) demonstrated:** Frugality, Deliver Results, Earn Trust
*Role guideline: "You work with partner teams to understand their roadmap and reach consensus on approach in order to deliver to meet business goals."*

**Example 2: Operationalizing GenAI into Production Operations — the Flash of Record and the Redirect Decision-Tooling Suite**

**In late 2025 — when sanctioned AI tooling was chatbot-only, before any CLI- or IDE-based tools existed in the org — William adopted the Context Document methodology and used it to take GenAI where it had not yet gone: governed, test-guarded production tooling the team runs as its mechanism of record.** Two builds anchor it. First, the daily IPEX Ops Flash: in H1 2025 it was hand-assembled each afternoon from dashboards, exports, and a steering doc — a ~2-hour combined build across multiple network owners. William rebuilt it into a GenAI-assisted pipeline that generates the network-steering narrative in minutes — **roughly 500 hours a year of combined manager capacity returned** — with every recurring call now an explicit rule. Anchored by an internal wiki he wrote and owns, it ran through a teammate's May 2026 coverage without him.

Second, he extended the pattern into a redirect decision-tooling suite that turns the team's vetting, costing, and audit judgment into deterministic code: a **redirect validator** — a five-criterion warrant gate (three giver-side, two taker-side) returning PASS/FAIL where SIM vetting once ran on individual judgment, with a self-inflicted-backlog check that catches redirects driven by planned-in receipts before they consume budget; a **cost estimator** on trailing 60-day per-lane actuals, so cost is checked before approval, protecting the $36.3M budget; and a **SIM auditor** that reconciles executed SIMs against the steering tracker, catching mis-booked levers and phantom-zero execution manual review missed. The suite is version-controlled and **test-guarded (1,100+ automated tests)** — AI-assisted code held to the same regression bar as any production mechanism — and ships as a shared team package with onboarding documentation (including Kiro-based) that teammates build from without him. He then **trained 11 stakeholders across IPEX on the methodology**.

**Leadership Principle(s) demonstrated:** Learn and Be Curious, Invent and Simplify, Ownership, Deliver Results
*Role guideline: "You identify priorities and know when a situation requires a deep dive vs. issues that can be solved quickly."*

**Example 3: Low-Cost Redirect Levers — Midstream Intermodal Reroute & Origin-Side Flip**

**When a mid-2026 Finance/ATS trailer-cap directive tightened the redirect-eligibility distance gate (1,000→750 miles) and cut redirect volume ~12%, William built new lower-cost levers that recover the freight where Amazon pays only incremental cost** — writing each lever's eligibility rules so the team runs them as standard levers. Redirect execution had effectively one mechanism (a full downstream re-move once freight reached a stressed node), so he found cheaper intervention points at both ends of the lifecycle:

- **AZIM 1P intermodal midstream reroute (in-transit).** Rather than wait for a load to arrive and pay a full downstream redirect, William reroutes it *while still on the rail leg* to a taker that needs the work — Amazon pays only the dray difference and avoids the full giver→taker over-the-road re-move. He wrote the reroutability logic himself — rail-tier, steel-wheel exclusion, standard-vs-crosstown, and chassis-zone gates that decide whether an in-transit load can still be diverted — and set up the request-and-execution process with the Amazon Intermodal (AZIM) team. Since WK24 the lever has redirected **323 in-transit loads / 5.39M units (~46/week)** for a **net transport avoidance of ~$137K to date — ~$820K annualized** (scaled to the seasonal redirect-volume curve, not a flat run-rate), priced load-by-load against carrier settlement actuals, with the avoided rail continuation on ramp-change flips measured from its canceled rail leg (conservative floor **~$109K**). **The principle carries to 3P intermodal (HJBI/PGLI) loads.**
- **OB NTI origin-side flip (pre-dispatch).** At the opposite end, William drove the outbound New-Transfer-In flip — redirecting transfer freight at the *origin* before a truck dispatches (nIXD → rIXD FC2), the cheapest intervention point — from an inconsistent break-glass action into a documented standard lever, its mechanics and eligibility agreed with Reactive Scheduling and Amazon Transportation Services (ATS).
- **TOFC over OTR — mechanism choice, executed.** The LAS1 Trailer-on-Flatcar (TOFC) teardown priced the same freight at **$2.6K/trailer railed via TOFC vs $6.6K over-the-road — ~$4K/trailer saved** (and ~$1.7K/trailer below incumbent LAS1 routing); the Q2 LAS1 program adopted it.

The playbook extends next to Amazon Freight External (AFE) freight, where an AFE midstream reroute is the next lever in build.

**Leadership Principle(s) demonstrated:** Invent and Simplify, Frugality, Are Right, A Lot, Deliver Results
*Role guideline: "You effectively manage difficult problems, mitigate immediate risks, and decide when you can handle or need to escalate."*

**Example 4: ICT Business-Review Architecture — Building the DBR→QBR Performance-Measurement System from Scratch**

**In his first six months at Amazon, William built ICT's daily-through-quarterly performance-measurement system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR) — a 49-plus-metric set, its ETL pipeline, and its IPEX Analytics publishing — that became the redirect-execution metric of record the steering org reads from.** When he joined, ICT read results ad hoc: no standard definition of on-time, no defect attribution, no daily-to-quarterly rhythm. He designed it end to end. The taxonomy spans execution, on-time pickup/delivery quality, and defect attribution by controllability (FC- vs IPEX- vs carrier- vs uncontrollable), broken out by network and mode, with the weekly grain adding lead-time-savings components. He did the hard analytical parts himself: correcting on-time logic for loads scheduled at a week boundary but picked up prior, excluding freight modes whose EDI gaps corrupted the signal, and re-basing transfer dwell to vendor-redirect dwell at hourly grain. When a source table stopped populating the load-creator field at the daily grain — which would have made it impossible to separate IPEX redirects from other aliases on the shared shipper account — he wrote the problem statement, escalated it, and drove the fix to a validated alternate table. Coordinating IPEX Analytics on the ETL build and IBWR on the workaround, he drove ICT-management sign-off and the quarterly-review gate. These reviews defined the metrics the flash automation (Example 2) later reads from: he built the measurement foundation, then automated the narrative on top of it.

**Leadership Principle(s) demonstrated:** Invent and Simplify, Ownership, Dive Deep, Deliver Results
*Role guideline: "You work with partner teams to understand their roadmap and reach consensus on approach in order to deliver to meet business goals."*

The organization already treats the scope as L5: an L8 director has tasked him directly, over his manager and senior peers, to own multi-site network cleanups; an L7 senior manager has publicly named him the redirect-execution authority; and he is a named action owner alongside L6 leaders in director sessions.

The proposed L5 role formalizes that scope and extends it in four directions:

1. **Multi-network strategic levers.** Example 3's lever portfolio is the template — turn a volume-cutting constraint into cheaper mechanisms and codify their gates. **3P Rate Matrix Expansion to HUBG and PGLI** (a Q3 2026 goal) is the next build, eliminating 95%+ of manual rate requests and cutting annual rate-request wait from ~200 hours to under 50.

2. **Codified judgment as scalable team tooling.** Extending Example 2's pattern across the rest of the reactive-steering surface — the WBR narrative, SIM-approval vetting, the redirectable and execution dashboards — is the direct continuation.

3. **Program-level cross-org partnership — the HJBI Drop-to-Live pilot in flight.** Identified and sized by the IPEX-VSCOR team in November 2025 at $22.5M annualized and stalled since, the pilot was re-engaged by William to live launch within three weeks of taking primary — pivoting to the HJBL SCAC-split when the original mechanism would have broken the carrier's drop first-pass yield — launching WK20, with the 2026 target held to $2.25M pending scalability read-out.

4. **Network-wide redirect standards and new-network execution.** William authored the IXD redirectability-feasibility intake for Amazon's network-wide redirect-automation program — the same rail-tier, standard-vs-crosstown, chassis-zone, and appointment-status logic from his own levers, generalized into the spec the automation validates against — and routed the per-network sign-off, proposed to the approving leaders. In parallel he executes redirects into networks that don't yet run on their own: pre-launch NVF test freight into the launching RDC network (IMO1, XLA4) and out-of-network SDC sites (HGR5, QXX6). The L5 role formalizes it: define what's redirectable, then stand up the execution wherever it lands.
