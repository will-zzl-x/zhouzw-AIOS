# William Zhou — L4 → L5 Promo Doc

William Zhou joined Amazon in October 2024 as a Supply Chain Manager I within Inbound Planning and Execution (IPEX). Within six months he owned redirects for two of five Regional Inbound Cross-Dock (rIXD) regions and had built ICT's daily-through-quarterly business-review system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR) from scratch; by Q3 2025 he owned all five rIXD regions, and in Q1 2026 he took on National IXD (nIXD). **Today, William owns all IXD redirect execution for IPEX-Inbound Control Tower (ICT) — 55 rIXD/nIXD sites (35 rIXD + 20 nIXD, as of July 2026) carrying ~450M units/week of inbound arrivals (WK28 2026 actual).**

The examples below show consistent L5 performance: he works ambiguous cross-functional problems with minimal to no direction, converts individual expertise and judgment into tools and standards the team runs, and independently drives consensus with internal and external partners on cross-org processes and initiatives.

# Scope of Role (L4 → L5)

*Scope is subject to change with the pending re-org to CIRS.*

The Supply Chain Manager II (SCMII) **IXD Redirect Execution** role within Inbound Control Tower (ICT) is an individual-contributor scope where the rules for daily reactive steering live as tribal knowledge. It demands command of constraint-level MDP, workload buffers, backlogs, and yard utilization to guide tactical stakeholders, own the response during escalations reaching Director-level visibility, and push back with data on senior asks that shift the problem rather than solve it — protecting against wasteful spend.

**The SCMII owns rIXD + nIXD redirect execution across 55 live rIXD/nIXD sites and ~450M units/week of inbound arrivals, within a 2026 redirect budget of $36.3M ($20.4M New Vendor Freight (NVF) inbound + $15.9M New Transfer In (NTI))** — extending forward to 1DC redirect escalations, RDC new-launch support (IMO1, ITX3), and special-project redirects for the Specialty (SDC) network (HGR5, QXX6). It holds internal and external partners (IPEX-VSCOR, IPEX-SKED, ESS, AFLTL, AWD, S&OP / WK0 Execution, Ops) to the same high data bar before aligning on redirects, and autonomously surfaces risky plans. It demands the ability to influence partner teams to commit bandwidth and funding toward network-health needs, backing every stance with data.

**Example 1: 3P Intermodal Rate Restructure — Cross-Functional Cost Methodology with J.B. Hunt Intermodal Pricing Leadership**

**William led the cross-functional restructure of 3P intermodal redirect pricing — converting a 2–4-hour-per-request spot-quote negotiation into a published, auditable standard that cut cost per move by a verified 47%.** In Q3 2025 these redirects ran on a spot-quote model that exposed Amazon to a Finance-estimated ~$2M in historical overpayment, with carriers double-paid for overlapping segments. Over 3+ months with J.B. Hunt Intermodal (HJBI) pricing leadership, Carrier Services, AP, and Controllership, he restructured the methodology so Amazon pays only incremental costs rather than the full lane: a foundational rate-publication agreement (published ramp-to-FC linehaul rates), then four weeks of iteration to a rate-difference-plus-administrative-fee structure that compensated HJBI while limiting Amazon to incremental costs — paired with an automated rate-lookup tool. It delivered **$79K in rate savings across its first 359 redirects ($229K projected annualized)** and now governs every 3P-IM redirect the team prices — **~$85K in estimated year-to-date rate savings**, re-verified in July 2026 against the published rate matrix.

**Leadership Principles demonstrated:** Earn Trust, Deliver Results
*Role guideline: "You work with partner teams to understand their needs and reach consensus on approach in order to deliver to meet business goals."*

**Example 2: Operationalizing GenAI into Production Tools — the Flash and the Redirect GenAI Suite**

**In late 2025 — when sanctioned AI tooling was chatbot-only, before any CLI- or IDE-based tools were approved for widespread non-technical use — William took the initiative to attend learning sessions on best practices such as the Context Document methodology, and used it both to apply and to teach how to take GenAI from an email editor to a trained, knowledgeable tool the team can use to create net-new capabilities.** He then held a training session for 11 stakeholders across IPEX. In 2026, as new-generation AI co-working tools like Claude and Kiro came online, he took this further by building multiple production outputs.

First, the daily IPEX Ops Flash: in H1 2025 it was hand-assembled each afternoon from dashboards, Excel outputs, and manual data entry — a ~2-hour combined build across multiple network owners. William rebuilt it into a GenAI-assisted pipeline that generates the network-steering breakdowns in minutes — an estimated **~500 hours a year of combined manager capacity returned.** Anchored by proper documentation, the workflow is scalable and has been operated successfully by teammates without his direct oversight.

Second, he extended the pattern into a suite of GenAI tools that turns the team's vetting and judgment into deterministic code. Notable examples: a **cost estimator** on trailing 60-day per-lane actuals (and additional criteria) so cost is quantified and surfaced to stakeholders for tradeoff-aware decisions; and a **SIM auditor** that reconciles open SIMs against updated backlog outlooks, ensuring no redirect spend goes to asks that are no longer warranted. The suite is version-controlled and regression-tested — AI-assisted code held to the same production discipline as any owned mechanism — and ships as a shared team package with onboarding documentation, so teammates can pull updates and refinements as they are patched.

**Leadership Principles demonstrated:** Learn and Be Curious, Invent and Simplify
*Role guideline: "You identify priorities and know when a situation requires a deep dive vs. issues that can be solved quickly."*

**Example 3: Low-Cost Redirect Levers — Midstream Intermodal Reroute & Origin-Side Flip**

**When a mid-2026 Finance/ATS trailer-cap directive tightened the redirect-eligibility distance gate (1,000 → 750 miles) and cut redirect volume ~12%, William built a portfolio of lower-cost levers that recover the freight at only incremental cost — and drove the hourly execution team to adopt a midstream / low-cost-first mindset over the path of least resistance.** Redirect execution had defaulted to a single mechanism — a full downstream re-move once freight reached a stressed node. William found cheaper intervention points across the inbound load lifecycle, wrote each lever's eligibility rules so the team runs them as standard work, and led the change management to make them stick: presenting the levers in team and all-hands forums, and partnering with Area Managers to cross-train associates (through the #3p-intermodal channel, with teammates Jamie and Sean executing) so midstream redirects became a feasible daily lever rather than a specialist's exception.

- **AZIM 1P intermodal midstream reroute (in-transit).** Rather than wait for a load to arrive and pay a full downstream redirect, William worked with the Amazon Intermodal (AZIM) team on a mechanism to redirect it *while still on the rail leg* to a taker that needs the work — Amazon pays only the dray difference and avoids the full giver→taker over-the-road re-move. He wrote the reroutability logic himself, after consulting AZIM on the criteria — the gates that decide whether an in-transit load can still be diverted. Since WK24 the lever has redirected **323 in-transit loads / 5.39M units (~46/week)** for a **net transport avoidance of ~$137K to date — ~$820K annualized** (scaled to the historical redirect-volume curve, not a flat run-rate), priced load-by-load against carrier settlement actuals, with the avoided rail continuation on ramp-change flips measured from its originally planned rail leg (conservative floor **~$109K**). The principle carries to 3P intermodal (HJBI / PGLI) redirects.
- **OB NTI origin-side flip (pre-dispatch).** William drove the outbound New-Transfer-In flip forward — redirecting transfer freight at the *origin* before a truck dispatches (nIXD → rIXD FC2) — from an inconsistent break-glass action into a documented standard lever, its mechanics and eligibility agreed with Reactive Scheduling and Amazon Transportation Services (ATS).
- **TOFC over OTR.** Leading two escalations to route freight via Trailer-on-Flatcar, William priced the mechanism against the over-the-road status quo — **~$4K/trailer saved on the direct-bypass routing, $2.3K/trailer on the executed LAS1/VGT2 lanes** — and the Q2 LAS1 program adopted it, moving **146 trailers / 2.6M units in its launch window for ~$336K in realized cost avoidance** before the lever was deliberately parked to hold the line on linehaul-reduction spend.

Upcoming work extends the playbook to Amazon Freight External (AFE) freight, partnering with GL and AF Finance to build a mechanism to reroute AFE appointments midstream — further reducing cost by avoiding the more expensive downstream Y2Y solution.

**Leadership Principles demonstrated:** Frugality, Are Right, A Lot
*Role guideline: "You effectively manage difficult problems, mitigate immediate risks, and decide when you can handle or need to escalate."*

**Example 4: ICT Business-Review Architecture — Building the DBR→QBR Performance-Measurement System from Scratch**

**In his first six months at Amazon, William built ICT's daily-through-quarterly performance-measurement system (DBR/WBR/MBR/QBR) — a 49-plus-metric set, its ETL pipeline, and its IPEX Analytics publishing — that became the redirect-execution metric of record the steering org reads from.** When he joined, ICT did not measure redirect performance and quality: no standard definition of on-time, no defect attribution, no daily-to-quarterly rhythm. He designed it end to end. The taxonomy spans execution, on-time pickup/delivery quality, and defect attribution by controllability (FC- vs IPEX- vs carrier- vs uncontrollable), broken out by network and mode, layered on top of the existing lead-time-savings components in the incumbent WBR. He did the hard analytical parts himself: correcting on-time logic for loads scheduled at a week boundary but picked up prior, excluding freight modes whose EDI gaps corrupted the signal, and re-basing transfer dwell to vendor-redirect dwell at hourly grain. When a source table stopped populating the load-creator field at the daily grain — which would have made it impossible to separate IPEX redirects from other aliases on the shared shipper account — he wrote the problem statement, escalated it, and drove the fix to a validated alternate table. He coordinated IPEX Analytics on the ETL build and the workarounds while driving final ICT-management sign-off.

**Leadership Principles demonstrated:** Ownership, Dive Deep
*Role guideline: "You work with partner teams to understand their roadmap and reach consensus on approach in order to deliver to meet business goals."*
