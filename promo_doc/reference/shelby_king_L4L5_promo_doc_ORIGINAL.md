*Current L4 Supply Chain Manager Role*
Inbound Planning and Execution (IPEX) orchestrates inventory flow from vendors and sellers into Amazon's fulfillment network across 386 facilities, managing appointment scheduling, carrier coordination, and capacity allocation. The team enables fulfillment centers to receive freight efficiently, directly impacting inventory availability, transportation costs, and customer delivery speed.

As a Supply Chain Manager at L4, this role supports tactical operational management by applying data analytics to identify gaps, establishing process standardization, and driving continuous improvement across workflows. At L4, the problem, opportunity, and strategy are defined by leadership. The role executes within established frameworks by building analytical tools (data pipelines, automated reports, dashboards), identifying operational patterns, and implementing straightforward process improvements. The role collaborates with peers and cross-functional teams including Retail, Finance, Transportation, and fulfillment operations to solve tactical issues, escalating complex decisions to senior leadership. Work is reviewed periodically and solutions may need refinement with guidance from senior team members. The role impacts team metrics around appointment quality, schedule utilization, and process efficiency.
 
*Proposed L5 Supply Chain Manager Role*
As a Supply Chain Manager supporting AWD Networks and Specialty Programs, this role owns operational performance across 26 buildings representing 5.03% of IPEX's Mean Days Processing Delay (MDP) and 6.7% of weighted workload, managing approximately 100,000 Inbound Shipment Appointments (ISAs) annually. MDPD—the unit-weighted delay between when freight is available for delivery until it is received—directly impacts Amazon's financial performance. Reducing MDPD across this portfolio yields approximately $2.6 million in annual benefits per day improved. Achieving the 18% year-over-year improvement target represents approximately $1.7 million in expected annual impact through inventory cost savings, enhanced delivery speed, and reduced redirect costs.

An L5 Supply Chain Manager's first responsibility is driving 18% year-over-year MDPD improvement across three distinct network types: AWD (Amazon Warehousing &amp; Distribution) Third-Party operations (6 sites, 3.5 days YTD MDP) providing bulk storage that automatically replenishes Prime-ready fulfillment centers for FBA sellers—delays here ripple directly to customer-facing stockouts; AWD First-Party operations (6 sites, 3.58 days YTD MDP) managing Amazon-owned inventory with different capacity models; and Specialty networks (14 sites, 4.31 days YTD MDP) handling inventory requiring customized solutions such as oversized items, hazmat, and temperature-controlled goods. The role ensures less than 10% of sites experience MDPD greater than 3 days, reducing variability to promote consistent performance for vendors and sellers.

An L5 Supply Chain Manager handles difficult operational problems where root causes and solutions may not be defined. When MDPD degrades, the role identifies whether performance decline stems from carrier behavior shifts, node-specific capacity bottlenecks, processing workflow failures, or systemic issues affecting multiple sites. Root cause analysis requires understanding vendor shipping patterns, carrier routing decisions, fulfillment center capacity constraints, and appointment scheduling algorithm behavior. The role decides independently whether issues can be resolved through process optimization or require escalation, building scalable mechanisms—automated monitoring, proactive alerting, standardized playbooks—to manage geographically distributed buildings.

An L5 Supply Chain Manager influences across teams and serves as Captain for Operational Forecasting Accuracy across the entire NA-US network, supporting METIS (the next-generation inbound planning system) implementation across all NA inbound cross-dock facilities by January 2026. This captainship extends beyond the 26 buildings under direct ownership to encompass forecasting quality for Amazon's full inbound network. Inaccurate forecasts cause capacity shortfalls leaving inventory stranded, wasted resources from over-allocation, and poor placement decisions increasing transportation costs. The role independently engages with fulfillment operations leaders, transportation planning teams, retail inventory managers, data science teams, and Supply Chain Optimization Technology (SCOT) engineers. The role publishes weekly scorecards showing forecast accuracy by metric type, network, and time horizon; identifies systematic bias before it impacts operations; and feeds insights to the L6 Senior Program Manager for strategic model refinement. As Secondary Captain for the Delta-to-2 MDP Reporting Tool, the role owns the early warning system for networks approaching 2.0 MDP threshold, providing cross-channel mitigation recommendations and tracking prevention effectiveness. The role consolidates forecast accuracy trends for leadership reviews and mentors others on forecasting methodologies.
