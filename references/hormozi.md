## Core definitions (the load-bearing vocabulary)

- **Learning = same circumstance, different behavior.** Knowledge that doesn't change behavior is not learning. A semester (or a quarter, or a reflection cycle) where circumstances and behavior both held constant = nothing was learned.
- **Intelligence = rate of learning.** How fast you change behavior under the same conditions. Not academic aptitude — adaptation speed.
- **Leverage = output ÷ input.** The whole game is trading up: fewer inputs, more output. **Decisions are the highest-leverage acts in a life** — that's why decision quality compounds harder than effort quality.
- **Validation = a stranger hands you money.** Not a survey, not a compliment, not a plan. Money from someone with no social reason to please you.

## Decision-making: risk-adjusted return with FINITE tries

1. For each option: what's the upside, and what's the honest probability of getting it?
2. Expected-value math assumes unlimited tries. **You don't have unlimited tries** — a 1-in-10 shot at 100x is a great portfolio bet and a terrible one-of-one bet if you only have 3 coins.
3. **The more you NEED the win, the higher-probability the bet you should take.** Stack high-probability wins first; take bigger swings only once you no longer need them to hit.
4. Corollary (Bezos, paraphrased): business has long-tail payoffs — sometimes a hit scores 1,000 runs. That's why staying in the game (not blowing up on one swing) dominates.

## The debt taxonomy — every business starts in debt

All businesses start in debt; the only question is which kind: **financial, ignorance, management, technical.**

- **Ignorance debt is the big one.** "Why aren't you making $10M/yr? Because you don't know how. That's a $10M/yr tax you pay annually." Paying it down = doing the thing many times, fast.
- **Never stack financial debt on top of ignorance debt — they compound.** First-time operators shouldn't borrow. (Buffett: "If you're smart you don't need it; if you're dumb you shouldn't use it.")
- Hormozi's famous $100k credit-card bet was NOT a leap of faith: 6 gyms + 30 turnarounds of paid-down ignorance debt first. The only real risk was payment processing. **Model how he got there, not where he is** — copying the late-stage behavior (big swings, jets) without the early-stage reps is how people blow up.
- Agencies/courses/mentors = **trading money for ignorance-debt paydown.** Legitimate when: tons of real reviews, operator has done it a long time, and you expect to get *directionally better*, not rich on the first try.

## Picking what to do

- **Use what you got** — the first rule. Start from domain exposure you already have (career, family industries, problems you've personally suffered). Passion follows competence; you can't be passionate about things you're bad at, and you're bad at everything you haven't done.
- **Fallacy of the perfect pick:** the business you start/buy now will NOT be your forever business. Entrepreneurs sprint in **3–5 year seasons**. Optimize for *stacking* — the next thing should build on the skills of the last, or you restart your domain-knowledge clock.
- **Be directionally correct, then iterate.** You lack the perspective to judge between plausible-looking options (the "which diet article is right" problem). Pick a defensible direction, move, and let contact with reality generate the information that picking never could.
- **You don't need to be original.** "Just do what someone else does, faster or easier. Both = charge more." Respect incumbents (the 20-location mechanic is not an idiot) — model first, iterate after you can replicate.

## Execution: the bar is on the floor

- **Most people don't try.** Beating half the field = answer the phone, respond to emails, tell people about the thing, do a good job, ask for the review. "A lot of crushing it in business is doing the basics other people won't."
- **Rule of 100:** 100 primary actions (reachouts / cold contacts / minutes of content / $ of ads), every day, for 100 days → you will have a business. Nobody does it — which is exactly why it works.
- **~1% is the cold-activity benchmark** (100 reachouts → a sale-ish). Expect to run 3x worse than benchmark at first; that's the ignorance debt amortizing.
- **Success is a character trait (consistency), not intelligence.** Real money took him 5 years of 80-hour weeks. Everything takes longer than expected; the person who keeps changing course guarantees they never arrive. "Eat less, move more, do it for a long time — the doing-it-every-day part is what people fuck up."

## The 8 promotion channels (complete list — there are no others)

**Solo (you do it):**
1. **Warm outreach 1:1** — people who know you. Don't pitch them; ask "do you know anybody who…" (referral ask converts warmth without sleaze).
2. **Content 1:many** — post publicly about what you do, for a long time.
3. **Cold outreach 1:1** — calls, emails, door knocks.
4. **Paid ads 1:many** — any platform.

**Leveraged (others do it):**
5. **Customer referrals** — systems + incentives + a genuinely good product.
6. **Employees** — systematize your 4, hire someone to run them at a margin.
7. **Agencies** — buy the skill to shortcut the learning curve (money for ignorance debt).
8. **Affiliates** — other businesses hand you their customers (the massage-in-every-new-hire-packet play). Most advanced, most powerful. ⚠️ When YOU are the affiliate/audience-holder: **the audience is the asset — never sell access in a way that transfers the list itself.**

**Sequencing rule: start with warm outreach, not ads** — you hear real people describe their problems in their own words, which becomes your copy, your offer, and your trust-builder later.

## The validation loop (free-first)

1. Do it **free for the first 5–20**. (You can't sell yet; free removes the skill barrier and banks emotional wins + testimonials.)
2. Do a good job → ask for a review + a referral.
3. Ask them to buy. **If no — ask why.** The answer reveals the **hidden costs**: not price, but the time/effort/sacrifice the buyer incurs around your thing. Fix those.
4. Re-ask. Iterate until demand exceeds capacity → **the supply/demand curve flips and you name your price.** (The influencer model is just this at scale: massive demand vs. tiny supply.)

## Partnerships (his 8 failed ones distilled)

- Partner ONLY for **money, skill, or time you don't have.** If both sides bring the same things, one of you is unnecessary — and you just halved everything you'll ever make, "one of the most expensive decisions you will ever make."
- **The other person must credibly double the value of the business** to justify 50%. If you can afford to *pay* for the help instead, keep 100% and hire.
- Same-everything partnerships end in mutual resentment: both will believe they work harder.

## Psychology: fuel, fear, confronting the work

- **Use whatever fuel you have** — anger, shame, pride, a great upbringing, whatever. Motivation doesn't need to be noble; it needs to be *yours*. Blaming circumstances hands the power for your outcomes to the people you resent.
- **"Fear of failure" is really fear of 1–3 specific people's opinions.** Name the person. Then ask: "Is [person]'s opinion the reason I'm not going to do what I want?" Named, it usually collapses.
- **Confront the work:** the $5k website he resented for years took 4 hours to learn to rebuild — "it took me four years to decide to take the first four hours." The tactics are rarely hard; the emotional toll and the consistency are the hard parts.
- You don't have to *deserve* success: "skip the whole message to yourself about whether you deserve it — just do the stuff that creates it."

## Quick reference — when to invoke Hormozi

- "Should I partner / what's a fair split?" → money/skill/time filter + double-the-value bar
- "Buy vs. build vs. new idea?" → debt taxonomy + finite-tries risk calibration
- "How do I grow [the business]?" → 8 channels, warm-first sequencing
- "Is this idea validated?" → a stranger handed you money, nothing else counts
- "I keep not doing X" → name the person; confront the work (4-years/4-hours)
- "Am I making progress?" → learning = behavior change under same circumstances; count reps, not knowledge
- Offer/pricing/unit-economics questions on a target business → hidden-costs lens (time/effort/sacrifice, not just price)
