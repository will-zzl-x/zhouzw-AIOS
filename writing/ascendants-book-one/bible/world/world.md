## History that matters now

- **The Logai genocide.** Part of Vane's rise to power was an attempt to exterminate the Logai, a race that held total, inherited knowledge of the past. Only Tessa and her twin sister survived; Vane captured the twin and has kept her ever since. Tessa severed whatever let the sisters' knowledge stay shared, so their knowledge has diverged since separation. *(Flagged in conversation as possible Book 4 prequel material.)*
- **The twin sister built Vane's pure-blood categorization/gift-analysis system** — the same system the prologue shows in action. Laila is the first subject it has ever failed to classify.
- **James's origin.** His father, a Valsk clan leader, kidnapped a half-breed woman intending to breed a pure-blood weapon. James was born with green eyes and no gold, initially dismissed as "giftless" — a dud. His father experimented on him trying to force a gift out (mechanism/extent not yet detailed), eventually gave up, and moved to kill both James and his mother. At that moment James's Extraction/Beast manifested for the first time, killing much of the camp, including the father. James and his mother (whom he calls "mom") escaped and later joined the Rogue. James is the only pure-blood the Rogue has ever taken in. His mother is alive and now serves as a nanny in the Rogue's nursery, caring for young children the Rogue finds.
- **The half-brother's facility.** In a later chapter, James is captured and tortured by a man who calls him "brotha" and references their father's formula — his half-brother (same father, different mother — no relation to James's mother's story), continuing the father's experiments on halfling children with the same aqua-blue-vialed formula. James did not know this brother was alive before the confrontation. Laila infiltrates the facility during this and rescues several of the children while James is held captive. She originally plans to save only a few children, but ends up also saving Ash — driven by her own childhood of being turned away and abandoned. See `ash.md`. **The other rescued children were stolen directly from the Rogue; Ash is the only one who wasn't — he was found on the street instead, and was given his name by the other child prisoners during captivity.** That the facility has specifically been taking children from the Rogue is a significant, unresolved thread — see Open Questions.
- **The Rogue reunion.** James and Laila return the rescued children to their families — all except Ash, who stays with them (he has no family from the Rogue to return to; found on the street, not stolen). At the Rogue, James reunites with his mother, and Laila watches, unguarded, for the first time. Overwhelmed by it, she turns away from Ash even as he calls out to her — she doesn't mention any of this to James in the moment, though it surfaces later as their bond deepens.
- **The Rogue's reception of Laila is split, and it's a genuine point of contention between members.** She bears "the maker's mark" — actually the tracker's marking gift, placed on her at some point in Vane's custody — visible on her neck. Since no gift functions on her, it's functionally inert (he can't actually track her through it), but the Rogue doesn't know that; many members are wary, believing the Guard can find Laila through the mark and, through her, find the Rogue itself. Against that, the families who just got their children back want to welcome her. Everyone trusts James, and James trusts Laila, which carries real weight — and Eryx's entire reason for extracting her in the first place was believing the Rogue was the safest place for her outside Vane's territory. **It's ultimately Tessa who allows Laila in**, overriding the wary faction as the Rogue's leader.
- **The man who delivered young Laila to Vane is the same pure-blood tracker/bounty hunter Vane later sends after her at Book 1's climax.** He has gold eyes; young Laila, having been turned away by everyone else, believed she'd finally found someone like her who would care for her. Instead he delivered her straight into Vane's custody. Years later, Eryx clones Laila to confuse this same tracker, and James extracts his gift (doesn't kill him, just depowers him) — the act Vane watches through the tracker's eyes, learning of James's existence. Whether Laila or James know this personal history at the time of that confrontation is still open.

## Rules of daily life

- Ascendants are born randomly to human parents; most are abandoned in infancy out of fear.
- A modern system (exact shape undecided — reward vs. mandate) encourages humans to hand pure-blood Ascendants over to Vane's regime.
- Gold-eye purity is the universal, visible classification method society uses to sort Ascendants: full gold = pure-blood, partial/sliver/single eye = half-breed, no gold = presumed human.
