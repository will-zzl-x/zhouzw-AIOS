## History that matters now

- **The Logai genocide.** Part of Vane's rise to power was an attempt to exterminate the Logai, a race that held total, inherited knowledge of the past. Only Tessa and her twin sister survived; Vane captured the twin and has kept her ever since. Tessa severed whatever let the sisters' knowledge stay shared, so their knowledge has diverged since separation. *(Flagged in conversation as possible Book 4 prequel material.)*
- **The twin sister built Vane's pure-blood categorization/gift-analysis system** — the same system the prologue shows in action. Laila is the first subject it has ever failed to classify.
- **James's origin.** His father, a Valsk clan leader, kidnapped a half-breed woman intending to breed a pure-blood weapon. James was born with green eyes and no gold, initially dismissed as "giftless" — a dud. His father experimented on him trying to force a gift out (mechanism/extent not yet detailed), eventually gave up, and moved to kill both James and his mother. At that moment James's Extraction/Beast manifested for the first time, killing much of the camp, including the father. James and his mother (whom he calls "mom") escaped and later joined the Rogue. James is the only pure-blood the Rogue has ever taken in. His mother is alive and now serves as a nanny in the Rogue's nursery, caring for young children the Rogue finds.
- **The half-brother's facility.** In a later chapter, James is captured and tortured by a man who calls him "brotha" and references their father's formula — his half-brother (same father, different mother — no relation to James's mother's story), continuing the father's experiments on halfling children with the same aqua-blue-vialed formula. James did not know this brother was alive before the confrontation. Laila infiltrates the facility during this and rescues several of the children while James is held captive. She originally plans to save only a few children, but ends up also saving Ash — driven by her own childhood of being turned away and abandoned. See `ash.md`. **The other rescued children were stolen directly from the Rogue; Ash is the only one who wasn't — he was found on the street instead, and was given his name by the other child prisoners during captivity.** That the facility has specifically been taking children from the Rogue is a significant, unresolved thread — see Open Questions.
- **The Rogue reunion.** James and Laila return the rescued children to their families — all except Ash, who stays with them (he has no family from the Rogue to return to; found on the street, not stolen). At the Rogue, James reunites with his mother, and Laila watches, unguarded, for the first time. Overwhelmed by it, she turns away from Ash even as he calls out to her — she doesn't mention any of this to James in the moment, though it surfaces later as their bond deepens. **Working idea, not fully locked:** she later goes back to check on Ash quietly while he's asleep, and separately has a conversation with James's mother about him that gives Laila real perspective — see `james-mother.md`.
- **The Rogue's reception of Laila is split, and it's a genuine point of contention between members.** She bears "the maker's mark" — actually the tracker's marking gift, placed on her at some point in Vane's custody — visible on her neck. Since no gift functions on her, it's functionally inert (he can't actually track her through it), but the Rogue doesn't know that; many members are wary, believing the Guard can find Laila through the mark and, through her, find the Rogue itself. Against that, the families who just got their children back want to welcome her. Everyone trusts James, and James trusts Laila, which carries real weight — and Eryx's entire reason for extracting her in the first place was believing the Rogue was the safest place for her outside Vane's territory. **It's ultimately Tessa who allows Laila in**, overriding the wary faction as the Rogue's leader.
- **The man who delivered young Laila to Vane is the same pure-blood tracker/bounty hunter Vane later sends after her at Book 1's climax.** He has gold eyes; young Laila, having been turned away by everyone else, believed she'd finally found someone like her who would care for her. Instead he delivered her straight into Vane's custody. Whether Laila or James know the tracker's personal history with her at the time of the climax confrontation is still open.
- **The training-exercise assassination attempt — a Book 1 midpoint, and James's first extraction of a person's gift.** The tension over letting Laila into the Rogue isn't just talk — a splinter group of halflings who disagree with Tessa's decision try to actually take Laila out. It happens during a Rogue training exercise (a two-sided game — something like capture-the-flag or a maze course — where Laila's role is to navigate her team from one point to another without being seen; James, Eryx, and others are also part of it). The opposing group uses the exercise as cover for a real attempt on her life. **Laila essentially lets it happen** — her Sight tells her James will intervene at the last moment to save her, and she's curious how he'll do it, so she doesn't fully defend herself. James extracts the attacker's gift rather than killing him — his first-ever on-page use of the ability, turned on one of his own.
- **The trial, and exile.** This scene is a big enough deal within the Rogue that Tessa calls a formal trial to determine what actually happened. Through it, the wider Rogue community learns two things at once: what Laila actually did (that she let the attack happen, essentially baiting the outcome, out of curiosity), and what James actually is (his Extraction gift and its true nature/danger, presumably not common knowledge before this). **This creates real conflict between James and Laila** — in effect, she baited him into becoming exactly the person he never wanted to be. **Working idea:** the trial's outcome is that James and Laila are cast out of the Rogue.
- **The Guard's raid and Book 1's climax.** While exiled, James and Laila learn of an incoming raid by Vane's forces on the Rogue and return to warn them, ultimately helping the Rogue fight off the attack and escape. During the raid, **Laila sets up a similar trap** to the training-exercise incident — Eryx clones her to confuse the tracker/bounty hunter sent after her — and James extracts the tracker's gift. This is James's **second** extraction of a person's gift. Two major consequences: **the mark on Laila's neck disappears** (it was sourced from/maintained by the tracker's gift, so losing his gift severs it), and **Vane loses control over all the pure-bloods marked by the tracker who are outside his headquarters area** — not a large number, but a real, meaningful slip in his total control. Vane watches the extraction through the tracker's eyes and learns of James's existence. **This is the hook into Book 2: Vane hunts James and Laila directly**, both for what James is and for the crack this opened in his control.

## Rules of daily life

- Ascendants are born randomly to human parents; most are abandoned in infancy out of fear.
- A modern system (exact shape undecided — reward vs. mandate) encourages humans to hand pure-blood Ascendants over to Vane's regime.
- Gold-eye purity is the universal, visible classification method society uses to sort Ascendants: full gold = pure-blood, partial/sliver/single eye = half-breed, no gold = presumed human.
